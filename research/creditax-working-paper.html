CREDITAX.AIABSTRACT
Abstract

Creditax.ai is a free-to-start platform that bridges Nigerian tax compliance and creditworthiness. A user signs up, asks tax questions and receives answers grounded in published Nigerian law (the Nigeria Tax Act 2023, the Nigeria Tax Administration Act 2023, and FIRS guides), calculates PAYE, VAT, withholding and company income tax with citable figures, sees a credit health score built from compliance and financial data, and, when human help is needed, is referred to a verified tax professional inside our own marketplace.

The founding insight: timely filing and consistent tax payment are among the strongest signals of financial reliability, yet no credit product in the Nigerian market treats compliance as a scoring signal. Bank-statement scoring misses the taxpayer whose paperwork is clean but whose banking history is thin. Creditax closes that gap, and in doing so manufactures a proprietary dataset no competitor holds: verifiable compliance behaviour, linked across tax types, languages, and professions.

The product ships in four languages (English, Yoruba, Hausa, Igbo) across a four-tier, Naira-only pricing model with tight free limits and a conversion target of one paid conversion per ten active free users within ninety days. Ten guided Skills (from withholding-tax credit recovery to employer compliance badges) grow revenue without growing interface complexity. A six-month, ₦10,000,000 Phase 1 delivers the core platform; a 24-week, six-phase roadmap ends in public launch.

PART I: CONTEXT
Where the idea came from
The 2024 reform created the questions; the credit market left the answers unpriced. At a glance: the 2023 reform acts, 7.5% VAT, 5-30% withholding, 17 income tax bands, and a compliance-to-credit bridge that exists nowhere in the market today.

1 · Introduction and Origins

Creditax began with two observations made in the same quarter of 2026.

First, the reform. Nigeria's 2023-2024 tax reform package (the Nigeria Tax Act 2023, the Nigeria Tax Administration Act 2023, and Capital Gains Tax amendments) rewrote brackets, rates, and filing procedure. Every employee, freelancer, trader, and small business owner woke up to questions the old materials no longer answered: what do I owe now, by when, and on what authority. Public guidance exists (FIRS publishes guides and circulars), but it is written for practitioners, not for the nurse in Surulere or the POS-agent in Kano.

Second, the scoring blind spot. Nigerian credit scoring runs on bank transaction data. That data captures spending and income flow, but it never sees the tax file. A trader with six years of clean filings and zero bank debt is scored thin; a salaried worker who has never filed is scored on salary alone. Compliance behaviour, the single most legible and verifiable signal of financial discipline a Nigerian adult generates, is simply not priced.

Creditax exists to fix both at once: make tax legible to the person who owes it, and make that person legible to the lender who serves them.

What Creditax keeps, and what it replaces

The old way (2026 status quo)The Creditax way
Generic chatbots that answer world-tax questions with no sourceAnswers restricted to published Nigerian law, with a citation on every response

2 · The Global Tax-Technology Landscape

Tax software at global scale has proven one thing: when guidance is authoritative and calculation is deterministic, consumers pay for it. TurboTax serves tens of millions of filers a year. Stripe Tax prices billions in cross-border transactions. Intuit, H&R Block, and a generation of fintech rails were built on the same recipe: rules maintained centrally, answers users can trust, filing that finishes in an afternoon.

None of that machinery has landed on the compliance-credit bridge, and none of it is built for Nigeria.

Why the gap persists

Tax content is jurisdictional, perishable, and high-stakes. A generic assistant that answers a Lagos VAT question with foreign or outdated rules is worse than no assistant at all, because the user cannot tell the difference until it costs them a penalty. Maintaining correct Nigerian rules is expensive, which is exactly why it is defensible: the platform that does it owns the moat.

The unclaimed-credit problem (the wedge)

Clients deduct withholding tax from vendor payments. Vendors who never claim the credit notes forfeit real money, amounts sitting on the books of firms that paid them. Skill 01 (Section 7) turns that forfeited balance into the platform's highest-revenue-first bet: a 5% success fee on recovered credits, filed through verified professionals.

The wedge is not "file your taxes." The wedge is: there is money you already earned that was withheld and never credited back to you. Here is the schedule to recover it.

Design posture

  • Nigeria-only depth before any regional breadth
  • Free delivers one genuine result, then quotas convert; BVN is never free at steady state
  • Every price in Naira, everywhere, including pitch materials

4 · The Tax Assistant

The assistant answers questions by retrieving relevant passages from the knowledge base of Nigerian tax law and assembling a response that names its sources. The system is built on a discipline, not a trick: an answer without a citation is not shipped. Responses that fall below the confidence bar carry a low-confidence flag and route the user to a verified professional instead of guessing.

  • Single conversational thread per topic, in the user's chosen language
  • Every answer cites NTA, NTAA, FIRS guide, or circular material
  • High-stakes matters (audit, objection, appeal) trigger mandatory referral
  • Answers carry the user's locale, so money phrasing and citations follow the language

The flywheel

  1. Compliance generates signal. Every calculation, filing estimate, and verified document is a data point about behaviour.
  2. Signal strengthens the score. Consistent filing and timely payment move the number.
  3. Better score improves access. Lenders and partners read the factor breakdown, not just the number.
  4. Access drives usage. Users return to keep the streak; cooperative and employer Skills manufacture verifiable income for the whole group.

8 · The Knowledge Base and the Verified Marketplace

Knowledge base

The knowledge base is a structured library of Nigerian tax material, maintained in a format that non-engineers can edit and that re-indexes when a document changes:

  • P0 (launch-critical): Nigeria Tax Act 2023; Tax Administration Act 2023; FIRS VAT Guide; personal income tax schedules
  • P1: Withholding tax circulars; Capital Gains Tax material; latest FIRS public rulings
  • P2: State IRS guides (Lagos first); tax appeal tribunal decisions; industry guides

Every article ships only after review by a licensed tax practitioner and a Content Editor. The admin console tracks version history, publish state, and re-indexing, with an audit log of every change.

The marketplace

There is no official public CAC API. Verification is therefore layered, not single-source:

  1. The professional enters their CAC registration number.
  2. Mono's Lookup API validates the business name and status.
  3. The professional uploads the CAC certificate as documentary proof.
  4. An administrator reviews the queue and approves or rejects.
  5. Approved profiles appear in the marketplace with the verified badge.

Discovery combines a browsable grid (filter by city, service, rating; sorted by distance when the user shares location) with natural-language matching through the assistant ("I need a VAT expert near Lagos Island"). Contact details (phone, email, WhatsApp) render only to logged-in users and are excluded from public pages, so the directory cannot be scraped.

18 · Regulatory and Compliance

Compliance is a gate, not a backdrop. The demo track (seeded data only) proceeds while registrations run in parallel; no real user BVN, bank link, or filing goes live before the gate items complete.

WhenItem
NowCAC company registration; lawyer engaged
During demo buildDPIA-lite review (mock data only); draft privacy notice (English)
Before productionNDPC registration (data controller of major importance); full DPIA; Data Protection Officer appointed (may be outsourced, on paper); four-language notices; licensed-pro review live

The two bright lines

  • The FIRS line. No scraping or unofficial integration of any government system. All content derives from published law and guidance, cited per answer. The agent gives general information, never representation; high-stakes matters trigger referral.
  • The CBN line. Not a licensed credit bureau, never presented as one. The score is indicative, for education and self-improvement. Bureau data enters only via partnership, as their report. No lending from our own balance sheet.

Breach response: assess, contain, notify NDPC and users within 72 hours.

Conversion below targetMEDIUMQuota walls name the exact limit; BVN teaser builds the habit; annual discount pulls cash forward; scenario bands (companion document) set expectations before spend
