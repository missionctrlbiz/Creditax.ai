# PAYE (Personal Income Tax) — How It Works

PAYE (Pay As You Earn) is the system under which an employer deducts
income tax from an employee's salary before payment and remits it to the
relevant tax authority.

## Who Pays PAYE

- Employees, civil servants, and other persons earning chargeable income
  from employment.

## Computation Steps

1. Start with **gross emoluments** (salary + taxable benefits).
2. Subtract **CRA** (the greater of ₦200,000 or 20% of gross, minimum
   1% of gross) and **statutory deductions** (pension, NHIS).
3. Apply the **graduated bands** to the chargeable income:

| Band | Chargeable income (₦) | Rate |
|------|-----------------------|------|
| 1 | First 300,000 | 7% |
| 2 | Next 300,000 | 11% |
| 3 | Next 500,000 | 15% |
| 4 | Next 500,000 | 19% |
| 5 | Next 1,600,000 | 21% |
| 6 | Next 2,400,000 | 23% |
| 7 | Above 5,600,000 | 24% |

4. The result is the annual PAYE; divide by 12 for the monthly figure.

## Under-Claimed Relief

Employees sometimes have their CRA or statutory deductions applied
incorrectly, leading to **overpaid PAYE**. A payslip decode can flag lines
that appear under-claimed and produce a lawful adjustment.

## Remittance

- The employer remits PAYE monthly by the **10th** of the following month.
- Employees can claim a refund where PAYE was overpaid.
