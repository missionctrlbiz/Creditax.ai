# Nigeria Tax Act 2023 — Key Provisions

## Personal Income Tax (PIT)

The Nigeria Tax Act 2023 replaced the Companies Income Tax Act (CITA) and the
Personal Income Tax Act (PITA) for the taxation of individuals and partnerships.

- Taxable income for individuals is computed under graduated bands.
- The Consolidated Relief Allowance (CRA) is the greater of **₦200,000** or
  **20% of gross income**, and must be at least **1% of gross income**.
- Resident individuals are taxed on worldwide income; non-residents on
  Nigeria-sourced income.

## Graduated Tax Bands (Residents)

| Band | Annual chargeable income (₦) | Rate |
|------|------------------------------|------|
| 1 | First 300,000 | 7% |
| 2 | Next 300,000 | 11% |
| 3 | Next 500,000 | 15% |
| 4 | Next 500,000 | 19% |
| 5 | Next 1,600,000 | 21% |
| 6 | Next 2,400,000 | 23% |
| 7 | Above 5,600,000 | 24% |

## Companies

- Standard corporate tax rate of **30%** for standard companies.
- Reduced rates apply for qualifying SMEs, agriculture, and specific sectors.
- Minimum tax applies to companies with no chargeable profit.

## Exemptions and Reliefs

- Exemptions include specified allowances, the CRA, and approved deductions.
- Statutory deductions (pension, NHIS) are deducted before computing chargeable income.

## Filing and Remittance

- Taxpayers must register and obtain a Tax Identification Number (TIN).
- PAYE is remitted by the withholding agent monthly; balance tax paid by
  31st October of the year following the assessment.

## Penalties

- Late filing and late payment attract graduated penalties.
- Default surcharges and interest apply to outstanding liabilities.
